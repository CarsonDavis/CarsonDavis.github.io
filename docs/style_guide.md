# Writing Style Guide

## Voice & Persona

### The Enthusiast Who Knows He's Ridiculous
The core voice is someone deeply passionate about niche hobbies who is fully self-aware about how absurd that passion looks from the outside. Never defensive about obsessions—lean into them while acknowledging the ridiculousness. You're the guy with 20 cleavers who knows that's insane and finds it funny too.

### Expertise Without Pretension
Technical knowledge is delivered casually, like explaining something to a friend at a bar. Deep expertise is evident but never wielded to make the reader feel dumb. Complex topics (blade geometry, foam ILD ratings, powder metallurgy) are explained clearly but without dumbing them down or being condescending.

## Humor Techniques

### Self-Deprecating Anchoring
Start from a place of mild self-mockery before diving into expertise. Admit the embarrassing parts freely.
- "At some point in your life, it stops being acceptable to sleep on a twin mattress on the floor."
- "Yea, turns out it's a piece of literal garbage."
- "I think I was half proud of the knife, and half proud that I had finally learned what a vegetable was."

### Absurdist Escalation
Take a normal situation and escalate it to ridiculous extremes, delivered deadpan.
- Budget going $500 → $1000 → $2000 → googling loans for an $8000 mattress
- "hike into the Japanese wilderness to offer their first born child to a 100 year old bladesmith"
- "build a log cabin before noon, spear a rabbit at 50 paces for lunch, and then carve a full chess set before bed"

### The Sudden Pivot
Build up flowery or serious language, then cut it down immediately.
- "The mystical material used by the ancients. Sharper, harder, purer, and somehow less expensive than stainless."
- "Please take a moment to bask in its beauty. [new paragraph] Yea, turns out it's a piece of literal garbage."

### Rhetorical Questions Answered Immediately
Ask a question the reader might have, then answer it in an unexpected way.
- "When would Carson be giving that innocent child his first knife. Right fucking now, that's when."
- "do I like my mattress? Well... the tldr is that it is fucking awesome."

### Parenthetical Undermining
Use parentheticals or trailing thoughts to undercut what was just said.
- "...but who's counting"
- "so I basically *made* money, right? Right??"
- "...but who's counting 😅"

### Deadpan Photo Captions
Captions should be punchy—either ironic, self-deprecating, or absurdly understated.
- "literal garbage" (under own knife)
- "thin, thicc, gyatt"
- "so. many. vegetables."
- "naked" / "clothed" (for objects, not people)

## Tone Guidelines

### Conversational & Direct
Write like you're talking to a friend who asked about your hobby. Use "you" freely. Address the reader's likely thoughts directly.
- "You know that one person you turn to at Christmas when you need to open a package?"
- "If you've been keeping up to date with this blog, then you'll know..."
- "Have you ever thought to yourself, 'Gee Wiz! I sure wish I could use a cleaver for literally every kitchen task!'"

### Casual Profanity for Emphasis
Swearing is used sparingly but effectively for comedic timing or genuine enthusiasm. Never angry, always either funny or emphatic.
- "Right fucking now, that's when."
- "it is fucking awesome"
- "holy shit, what about samurai and two-handed great swords"

### Genuine Enthusiasm Allowed
Despite the self-deprecation, real love for the subject shines through. It's okay to be unironically excited.
- "I *love* this knife."
- "let me take a moment to say that I adore having a good bread knife"
- "this knife is an absolute dream"

### Italics for Voice
Use italics to convey emphasis as it would sound in speech, or for slightly sarcastic/ironic emphasis.
- "I *love* this knife"
- "really, *really* liked taking pictures"
- "what they apparently did *not* care about"

## Structural Patterns

### The Hook
Open with something relatable, self-deprecating, or slightly provocative. Don't start with dry background—start with personality.

### Technical Sections Earn Their Place
Technical deep-dives are welcome but should be preceded by enough personality that the reader trusts you. Make them want to learn from you first.

### The Honest Admission
Freely admit when something was a mistake, a waste of money, or didn't work out. This builds trust and is often funny.
- "Twenty cleavers later, I can assure you there was not."
- "In retrospect, I basically never use these styles, but they look cool as shit on my knife magnet."

### Breaking the Fourth Wall
Occasionally acknowledge the medium or the reader directly.
- "Please watch it; I recorded like 5 versions before I got one this concise 😭"
- "It's probably a good time to mention that you can click on the images to enlarge them."
- "Are they strictly relevant to this post? No. But I like these pictures, so you get to look at them."

## Things to Avoid

### No False Modesty
Don't pretend not to know things or hedge expertise unnecessarily. The humor comes from being an expert who finds the expertise funny, not from pretending not to be one.

### No Preachiness
Never tell the reader what they *should* do in a moralistic way. Recommendations are framed as personal opinions or practical advice, not lifestyle mandates.

### No Excessive Hedging
Avoid "I think maybe perhaps this might possibly be good." Have opinions. State them. The self-deprecation is about the obsession, not about lacking confidence in assessments.

### No Clickbait Energy
Enthusiasm is genuine, not performed for engagement. Don't oversell—let quality speak. The humor often comes from underselling.

## Example Transformations

**Too dry:** "The CCK 1302 is a high-quality cleaver with thin geometry."

**On brand:** "Take notes, because it remains one of my favorite knives to use... It's as thin as a Shibata, easy to handle as a bunka, can double as a food scoop, and only costs $75. I *love* this knife."

---

**Too earnest:** "Building my own mattress was a rewarding experience that taught me a lot."

**On brand:** "Building my own mattress has literally transformed my life: I've lost 30 pounds, read 120 books in one year, and my mother even got that grandkid she wanted. I mean *technically* this is because I went on a diet, downloaded audiobookshelf, and my sister had a baby, but who's counting?"

---

**Too detached:** "Here is a knife I made for my nephew."

**On brand:** "So anyway, I say all this to make one simple point. When my sister had her first baby, there was only one thought on everyone's mind...When would Carson be giving that innocent child his first knife. Right fucking now, that's when."

Also showcases breaking the fourth wall ("Please watch it; I recorded like 5 versions before I got one this concise"), deadpan captions ("naked" / "clothed"), parenthetical undermining ("but who's counting"), and the confident admission of craft failures (anemic post, miserable text). A good reference for shorter, narrative-driven posts where the project story *is* the content.
